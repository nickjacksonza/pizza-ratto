# Pizza Ratto brand profile

## Positioning

Pizza Ratto is an independent wood-fired pizza restaurant in Salt River, Cape Town: Italian soul with street-corner attitude. It is handmade, funny, local and a little unruly, but never confusing or hostile.

## Voice

- Short, punchy and specific.
- Warm beneath the bravado.
- Serious about dough, fire, ingredients and timing.
- Never corporate, luxurious, generic or over-explained.
- Jokes can be irreverent, but transactional copy must remain immediately clear.

Representative lines already used by the brand include “Made fresh. Taken seriously by no one.” and “Pick your ride.” Future copy should feel compatible with those lines rather than imitating them mechanically.

## Visual character

- Warm paper support with approved orange, red, pink and charcoal-grey inks.
- Oversized condensed poster typography, paired with a practical geometric sans-serif for reading.
- Checkerboard bands, thin rules, imperfect borders, slight rotation and overlapping editorial compositions.
- The rat-on-Vespa illustration and hand-painted Pizza Ratto wordmark are primary brand assets.
- Food photography should feel tangible and cut out, not polished into generic delivery-app cards.
- Motion is concentrated in one memorable scroll-led Remi/Vespa sequence, with quieter photography parallax elsewhere. The identity still comes primarily from composition, texture and type.

The implemented palette and component rules are documented in [`design-system.md`](design-system.md). That file is authoritative for code changes; the flyer remains the creative reference.

## Brand characters

Remi is the rat mascot. Big Bertha is the wood-fired oven. The venue connects to vintage Vespa culture, and a mural was painted by local artist Buffy. Any fuller story needs owner approval before publication.

## Creative test

Before accepting a design change, ask:

1. Does it still look like it belongs beside the physical flyer?
2. Can a first-time visitor still find the menu, hours and order actions quickly?
3. Is the deliberate roughness controlled, or does the layout simply look broken?
4. Does it work at phone width without sacrificing essential art or tap targets?

The site should be “designed mess,” not accidental mess.
